# 🇳🇬 Nigeria Consumer Price Index (CPI) Analysis — 2022 to 2024

A data analysis project exploring Nigeria's inflation trends over a 3-year period using official data from the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS). Built with Microsoft Excel as part of a data analyst portfolio.

---

## The Question

**How has inflation evolved in Nigeria between 2022 and 2024, which categories have driven it most, and what patterns emerge from the data?**

Nigeria experienced a significant inflationary period during this window — shaped by global commodity shocks, fuel subsidy removal in mid-2023, and currency depreciation. This project uses publicly available CPI data to trace that story with numbers.

---

## Data Source

- **Publisher:** National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) Nigeria
- **Portal:** [nigerianstat.gov.ng](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng)
- **Coverage:** January 2022 – December 2024 (36 months)
- **Framework:** COICOP 2018 (Classification of Individual Consumption by Purpose)
- **Categories tracked:** Headline, Food, Core, Urban, Rural, plus 5 COICOP sub-categories

## Key Findings

1. **Headline inflation nearly tripled** — rising from 15.6% in January 2022 to a peak of 34.8% by December 2024, a 19.2 percentage point increase.
2. **Food inflation was the primary driver** — consistently outpacing headline inflation by 6–7 percentage points throughout the period, indicating persistent food supply and logistics pressures.
3. **The sharpest acceleration occurred in mid-2023** — following the removal of Nigeria's fuel subsidy in June 2023, month-on-month inflation surged above 3%, the highest single-month jump in the dataset.
4. **Core inflation also rose sharply** — from ~14% in early 2022 to ~29% by end-2024, confirming that price pressures were broad-based and not limited to food and energy.
5. **Urban inflation consistently exceeded rural inflation** — by an average of ~0.8 percentage points, reflecting higher urban exposure to import-driven prices and energy costs.
6. **Signs of moderation appeared in Q3 2024** — food inflation dipped from its March 2024 peak of 40%, though headline rates remained historically elevated.

## Workbook Structure

| Sheet | Contents |
|-------|---------|
| `1. Raw Data` | 36 months of raw NBS CPI data across 11 metrics with source documentation |
| `2. Cleaned Data` | Computed metrics: Food-Core gap, MoM acceleration, Urban-Rural gap, inflation category labels |
| `3. Analysis` | Annual average tables, peak/trough summary, COICOP category breakdown, written findings |
| `4. Dashboard` | KPI summary cards, line chart (Headline vs Food vs Core), bar chart (annual averages) |
